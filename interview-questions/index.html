    Product Lead Interview Questions and Answers¶

    179 questions with the correct answer and a short explanation, organised from beginner (Level 1) to advanced (Level 4). Every question comes from a lesson in the free Product Lead Mastery Path, so you can open the lesson to go deeper.

    Practise, don't just read

    Each lesson also ends with an interactive quiz, and the final quiz tests all four levels. Try to answer each question below before reading the answer.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    01 · What Is a Product Lead?¶

    Q1. What changes most when a PM becomes a Product Lead?

    Answer: Your output becomes the team's outcomes, not your own artefacts

    Leverage through others.

    Q2. A new lead keeps writing every PRD personally. What is the risk?

    Answer: They become a bottleneck and the PMs don't grow

    Delegate and coach.

    Q3. What is the lead's most important deliverable?

    Answer: A team that makes good product decisions without them

    Capability.

    Q4. Who owns the product outcome when a PM on your team misses?

    Answer: Both: the PM owns the work, the lead owns the system of support and clarity

    Shared accountability.

    02 · Leading a Product Team¶

    Q5. A team ships lots but nobody knows why. What is missing?

    Answer: Clear goals and outcome metrics tied to the product vision

    Output vs outcome.

    Q6. What does a good team operating rhythm include?

    Answer: Regular planning, reviews and retros with clear owners

    Predictability.

    Q7. How should you handle a PM who disagrees with a decision?

    Answer: Listen, explain reasoning, and commit as a team once decided (disagree and commit)

    Respect and clarity.

    Q8. What does psychological safety enable in a product team?

    Answer: Candid bad news and challenges early

    Risk surfaces sooner.

    03 · Setting Product Vision for a Team¶

    Q9. What makes a product vision useful?

    Answer: It describes a future customers want and guides trade-offs

    Direction.

    Q10. A vision statement says 'be the best platform'. What is wrong?

    Answer: Too vague to guide decisions; make it specific about who and what changes

    Testable.

    Q11. How test whether a vision works?

    Answer: Ask if it helps the team say no to a plausible idea

    Trade-off tool.

    Q12. Who should shape the vision?

    Answer: The lead with input from customers, engineers and design, then communicated widely

    Co-created, clear owner.

    04 · Delegating Product Decisions¶

    Q13. What should a lead delegate?

    Answer: Decisions the PM has context for and can reverse, with clear guardrails

    Delegation with boundaries.

    Q14. What is a two-way door decision?

    Answer: Easily reversible, so decide fast and delegate

    Bezos framing.

    Q15. You delegated a decision and disagree with the outcome. What do you do?

    Answer: Coach on the reasoning; only reverse if it breaches guardrails or is high-stakes irreversible

    Trust is built.

    Q16. What makes delegation fail?

    Answer: Unclear ownership and criteria for success

    State the outcome and constraints.

    05 · Cross-Team Alignment Basics¶

    Q17. Two teams depend on each other but have different priorities. First step?

    Answer: Make the dependency and trade-offs visible and agree on shared goals

    Alignment before escalation.

    Q18. What helps cross-team alignment?

    Answer: A shared goal, regular syncs and written decisions

    Clarity.

    Q19. When escalate?

    Answer: When teams can't resolve a trade-off after good-faith effort and the cost of delay is high

    Last resort with options.

    Q20. What's a common source of misalignment?

    Answer: Unstated assumptions about priority and ownership

    Write it down.

    06 · Mentoring PMs¶

    Q21. What is the best way to mentor a PM on discovery?

    Answer: Observe their customer calls and give specific feedback afterward

    Practice with feedback.

    Q22. What distinguishes coaching from telling?

    Answer: Asking questions that help them find the answer

    Builds judgement.

    Q23. A PM keeps asking you for answers. What do you do?

    Answer: Ask for their recommendation first, then challenge it

    Build autonomy.

    Q24. How should feedback be given?

    Answer: Timely, specific, about behaviour and impact

    Actionable.

    07 · Running Effective Product Rituals¶

    Q25. What is the purpose of a product review ritual?

    Answer: Shared decisions and learning, not status reporting

    Decision forum.

    Q26. A weekly meeting has become a status read-out. Fix?

    Answer: Send updates in writing and use the meeting for decisions and debate

    Async status.

    Q27. What does a good retro produce?

    Answer: A few owned actions the team will actually do

    Follow-through.

    Q28. How often should roadmap reviews happen?

    Answer: At a cadence matching decision speed, e.g. monthly/quarterly with weekly adjustments

    Fit to context.

    08 · Basic Org Design for Product Teams¶

    Q29. What is span of control for a Product Lead typically?

    Answer: About 5–8 direct reports

    Coaching capacity.

    Q30. What org structure aligns teams to customer outcomes?

    Answer: Cross-functional squads owning a part of the customer journey

    Autonomy.

    Q31. What signals a bad org structure?

    Answer: Constant handoffs and unclear ownership

    Fix boundaries.

    Q32. When reorganize?

    Answer: When the current structure blocks strategy, not for novelty

    Reorg cost is high.

    09 · Hiring PMs Basics¶

    Q33. What should you hire a PM for first?

    Answer: Evidence of judgement and outcomes, not just a polished résumé

    Structured assessment.

    Q34. Why use a scorecard in hiring?

    Answer: To compare candidates against the same criteria and reduce bias

    Consistency.

    Q35. What is a good interview question for PMs?

    Answer: Ask for specific past decisions and what they would do differently

    Behavioral.

    Q36. Who should debrief after interviews?

    Answer: Interviewers share written feedback independently before discussing

    Avoid anchoring.

    10 · Project — Product Team Charter & Rituals Plan¶

    Q37. What does a team charter contain?

    Answer: Mission, scope, ownership, decision rights, goals and ways of working

    Alignment doc.

    Q38. How make the charter live?

    Answer: Review it quarterly with the team and update it

    Living doc.

    Q39. What does 'decision rights' clarify?

    Answer: Who decides what and who is consulted

    RACI/DACI style.

    Q40. What is a sign the charter works?

    Answer: Fewer ownership disputes and faster decisions

    Outcome.

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    Advanced Product Team Leadership¶

    Q41. A high-performing PM is burning out. What do you do?

    Answer: Talk early, adjust load and expectations, and address causes in the system

    Sustainable pace.

    Q42. What builds trust with a team?

    Answer: Consistency, candor and following through

    Reliable behavior.

    Q43. What should a lead do when two PMs conflict?

    Answer: Facilitate directly: clarify interests, decision rights and agree on next steps

    Resolve early.

    Q44. What is a leader's role in change?

    Answer: Explain why, set direction and support people through transitions

    Communicate more than seems needed.

    Managing Multiple Product Squads¶

    Q45. How do you manage several squads with different needs?

    Answer: Set common goals and guardrails, then tailor support per squad

    Consistency with flexibility.

    Q46. How avoid being a bottleneck across squads?

    Answer: Build decision-making capability in PMs and use written principles

    Scale judgement.

    Q47. What does a portfolio view of squads show?

    Answer: Where investments sit versus strategy and where gaps are

    Resource fit.

    Q48. When rotate PMs between squads?

    Answer: For development or to resolve fit, with care for continuity

    Deliberate moves.

    Performance Management for PMs¶

    Q49. A PM misses expectations for a quarter, and this is the first time you've said so. What does that indicate?

    Answer: A management failure to give timely feedback

    Feedback early.

    Q50. What must every rating include?

    Answer: Evidence: at least two specific examples

    Defensible.

    Q51. What is calibration for?

    Answer: Aligning standards across managers so ratings are fair

    Consistency.

    Q52. What does a performance improvement plan need?

    Answer: Clear expectations, support, timeline and honest consequences

    Fair and clear.

    Cross-Functional Leadership¶

    Q53. How lead without authority across functions?

    Answer: Shared goals, clear decision rights and earning trust through outcomes

    Influence.

    Q54. Engineering and product disagree on scope. First step?

    Answer: Return to the user problem and success metric, then trade off options

    Shared goal.

    Q55. What helps with design/product tension?

    Answer: Early collaboration on problem framing and shared discovery

    Dual-track.

    Q56. What does a healthy product-engineering relationship need?

    Answer: Mutual respect for constraints and early involvement

    Partnership.

    Product Strategy Cascading¶

    Q57. What does strategy cascading mean?

    Answer: Translating company strategy into team goals each level can act on

    Line of sight.

    Q58. A team's goals don't connect to company strategy. Fix?

    Answer: Re-derive goals from strategy and drop work that doesn't connect

    Focus.

    Q59. How many top priorities should a team have?

    Answer: A small number (about 3)

    Focus.

    Q60. What do OKRs do well?

    Answer: Align teams on outcomes and make trade-offs visible

    Used well.

    Advanced Hiring & Interviewing for Product¶

    Q61. What should hiring loops test?

    Answer: The specific competencies in the role scorecard

    Structured.

    Q62. How reduce bias in interviews?

    Answer: Consistent questions, independent written feedback and diverse panels

    Process.

    Q63. What is a take-home exercise's risk?

    Answer: It can disadvantage candidates with less time; keep it short and relevant

    Fairness.

    Q64. What should an offer conversation include?

    Answer: Clear expectations, growth path and a prompt decision

    Close well.

    Coaching PMs on Discovery & Delivery¶

    Q65. What does coaching discovery involve?

    Answer: Reviewing interviews, assumptions and evidence with the PM

    Quality of learning.

    Q66. A PM only talks to customers who love the product. What do you coach?

    Answer: Seek disconfirming evidence and a representative sample

    Bias.

    Q67. How coach delivery?

    Answer: Focus on scope, trade-offs and communication with engineering

    Flow.

    Q68. How measure coaching impact?

    Answer: PM's independent decision quality improving over time

    Outcome.

    Managing Up¶

    Q69. What does managing up require?

    Answer: Clear communication of progress, risks and the decisions you need

    No surprises.

    Q70. Your boss asks for a feature you think is wrong. What do you do?

    Answer: Share evidence and alternatives, then commit if they decide

    Disagree and commit.

    Q71. How frame a request for resources?

    Answer: Link it to outcomes and trade-offs with options

    Business case.

    Q72. What builds credibility with executives?

    Answer: Delivering outcomes and being candid about risks

    Trust.

    Budget & Resourcing for Product Teams¶

    Q73. A budget cut forces a 20% reduction. How do you prioritize?

    Answer: Protect work most tied to strategy and customer outcomes, cut the rest explicitly

    Trade-offs.

    Q74. What is the fully loaded cost of a hire?

    Answer: Salary plus benefits, tools, overhead and management time

    Plan accurately.

    Q75. How decide build vs buy?

    Answer: Compare total cost, strategic importance and time to value

    Core vs context.

    Q76. How present headcount asks?

    Answer: Tie to outcomes, show alternatives and the cost of not hiring

    Business case.

    Project — Hiring & Team-Scaling Plan¶

    Q77. What does the hiring and scaling plan combine?

    Answer: Capacity model, hiring plan, interview process and onboarding

    Level 2 project.

    Q78. How know how many PMs you need?

    Answer: Map strategic bets to the capacity each needs and ratios to engineering

    Capacity planning.

    Q79. What should onboarding give a new PM?

    Answer: Context, relationships and an early win within 30–60–90 days

    Ramp.

    Q80. What do you track for hiring?

    Answer: Time to hire, offer acceptance, quality of hire and diversity

    Funnel.

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    Leading Product Leads (the Head of Product Role)¶

    Q81. What changes leading product leads?

    Answer: You manage through leads: set context and standards, and coach their management

    Second-order leverage.

    Q82. A lead under you avoids hard feedback. What do you do?

    Answer: Coach them on the conversation and hold them accountable for doing it

    Develop managers.

    Q83. How keep standards consistent across leads?

    Answer: Shared rubrics, calibration and reviews

    Calibrate.

    Q84. What is the main risk of removing a layer?

    Answer: Overloaded managers and less coaching

    Balance.

    Multi-Product Portfolio Leadership¶

    Q85. How allocate across a multi-product portfolio?

    Answer: By strategic role (grow, sustain, explore) and expected return

    Portfolio logic.

    Q86. A mature product consumes most resources but grows 1%. What do you consider?

    Answer: Rebalance toward growth bets while sustaining the core efficiently

    Investment mix.

    Q87. What belongs in portfolio reviews?

    Answer: Strategy fit, performance, risks and resource trade-offs

    Decisions.

    Q88. How decide to sunset a product?

    Answer: Weigh strategic value, usage, cost and migration path, with a communication plan

    Do it deliberately.

    Org Design at Scale¶

    Q89. What drives org design at scale?

    Answer: Strategy, customer journey and team interaction costs

    Structure follows strategy.

    Q90. What does Conway's law say?

    Answer: Systems mirror the communication structure of the organization

    Design teams to match the architecture you want.

    Q91. What is a stream-aligned team?

    Answer: A team aligned to a flow of work from a business domain

    Team Topologies.

    Q92. What is a reorg success measure?

    Answer: Faster decisions and clearer ownership within a few quarters

    Evaluate.

    Building Product Career Ladders¶

    Q93. What does a career ladder define?

    Answer: Expectations at each level by dimension with examples

    Clarity.

    Q94. A PM wants a promotion but is not at the next level. What do you do?

    Answer: Show the gap with evidence and a development plan

    Honest.

    Q95. How avoid title inflation?

    Answer: Promote on demonstrated sustained performance at the next level

    Standards.

    Q96. Why have an IC track?

    Answer: Senior craft can grow without becoming managers

    Dual ladder.

    Advanced Executive Communication¶

    Q97. How present to executives?

    Answer: Lead with the decision or answer, then key evidence

    Bottom line first.

    Q98. What do executives want from product updates?

    Answer: Outcomes, risks and decisions needed

    Relevance.

    Q99. How handle a surprise bad number?

    Answer: Share early with cause analysis and next steps

    Candor.

    Q100. What makes a good narrative doc?

    Answer: Clear problem, options considered, recommendation and risks

    Reasoned.

    Product Culture & Rituals at Scale¶

    Q101. How scale a product culture?

    Answer: Codify principles, hire and promote for them and model them consistently

    Behaviors.

    Q102. What kills culture?

    Answer: Leaders who don't follow the stated values

    Credibility.

    Q103. What are good culture signals?

    Answer: Decisions documented, candid reviews and customer contact

    Evidence.

    Q104. How handle a high performer who harms culture?

    Answer: Address behavior directly; keep standards even for stars

    Consistency.

    M&A Integration for Product Orgs¶

    Q105. What matters most in post-merger product integration?

    Answer: Clear customer-facing plan and decisions about overlap

    Customer first.

    Q106. How treat the acquired team?

    Answer: With respect and clear roles, retaining key talent

    Retention.

    Q107. What is a common integration mistake?

    Answer: Delaying decisions on overlapping products

    Ambiguity costs.

    Q108. How merge two roadmaps?

    Answer: Rebuild one strategy and prioritize across both with shared criteria

    Unified.

    Platform vs Product Team Structures¶

    Q109. When use a platform team?

    Answer: When many teams need shared capabilities and the platform is treated as a product

    Internal customers.

    Q110. A platform team builds what nobody uses. Why?

    Answer: It didn't treat product teams as customers or measure adoption

    Product mindset.

    Q111. What's a risk of a pure feature-team model?

    Answer: Duplicated work and inconsistent experience

    Balance.

    Q112. How decide the structure?

    Answer: Based on strategy, dependencies and where coordination costs are highest

    Contextual.

    Advanced Stakeholder Management (Board-Level)¶

    Q113. How manage board-level stakeholders?

    Answer: Prepare concise narrative, metrics and clear asks, with no surprises

    Trust.

    Q114. What does a board want to know?

    Answer: Strategy, performance against it, risks and capital needs

    Governance.

    Q115. A board member challenges your strategy. What do you do?

    Answer: Engage with evidence, acknowledge uncertainty and clarify trade-offs

    Open.

    Q116. How prepare for hard questions?

    Answer: Pre-wire key members and anticipate objections

    Preparation.

    Project — Org Design for a 20+ Person Product Org¶

    Q117. What does the org design project combine?

    Answer: Strategy, structure options, interaction model, transition plan and metrics

    Level 3 project.

    Q118. How evaluate structure options?

    Answer: Against criteria such as ownership clarity, speed and dependencies

    Decision matrix.

    Q119. What goes in a transition plan?

    Answer: Sequencing, communication, risks and support for people

    Humane.

    Q120. How measure success?

    Answer: Decision speed, delivery predictability and engagement

    Outcomes.

    Level 4 · Master¶

    VP/CPO-Level Strategy¶

    Q121. What does a VP/CPO own that a lead doesn't?

    Answer: Company-level product strategy and the org that executes it

    Scope.

    Q122. How set product strategy at CPO level?

    Answer: Choose where to play and how to win, then align portfolio and org

    Choices.

    Q123. What is the CPO's key partnership?

    Answer: CEO and CTO, aligned on strategy and execution

    Triad.

    Q124. What does a CPO say no to?

    Answer: Good ideas that don't serve the strategy

    Focus.

    Building Product Organizations from Scratch¶

    Q125. First step when building a product org from scratch?

    Answer: Clarify strategy and the first few roles that unlock it

    Sequence.

    Q126. Who do you hire first?

    Answer: Strong generalist leaders who can build the function

    Foundation.

    Q127. What process comes first?

    Answer: Lightweight rituals for decisions and priorities, added as needed

    Minimal viable process.

    Q128. What mistake is common?

    Answer: Copying a big-company model prematurely

    Fit stage.

    Product Leadership in Turnarounds¶

    Q129. What defines leadership in a turnaround?

    Answer: Clarity, speed and hard prioritization with honest communication

    Focus.

    Q130. What do you do first in a turnaround?

    Answer: Diagnose root causes and stop the bleeding

    Triage.

    Q131. How treat the team in a crisis?

    Answer: Be transparent and decisive, and support people

    Trust.

    Q132. What measure shows recovery?

    Answer: Leading indicators of customer value and retention

    Evidence.

    Board & Investor Relations for Product¶

    Q133. What do investors want from product leaders?

    Answer: A credible strategy, metrics and ability to execute

    Confidence.

    Q134. What is a good investor update?

    Answer: Honest metrics, wins, misses and asks

    Candor.

    Q135. How handle a missed plan?

    Answer: Explain the cause, the learning and the revised plan

    Credibility.

    Q136. What belongs in a board deck?

    Answer: Strategy, performance, risks and decisions

    Concise.

    Building Product Leadership Pipelines¶

    Q137. What is a leadership pipeline?

    Answer: A deliberate system to develop future leaders

    Succession.

    Q138. How spot future leaders?

    Answer: Look at judgement, influence and growth under stretch

    Potential.

    Q139. What develops leaders?

    Answer: Stretch assignments with coaching and feedback

    Experience.

    Q140. Why plan succession?

    Answer: Reduce risk when leaders leave and build bench strength

    Continuity.

    Global Product Org Leadership¶

    Q141. What changes leading a global product org?

    Answer: Time zones, cultures and local market needs

    Adapt.

    Q142. How balance global consistency and local needs?

    Answer: Global standards with local autonomy in defined areas

    Balance.

    Q143. What helps distributed teams?

    Answer: Written communication, clear decision rights and overlap hours

    Async.

    Q144. How avoid HQ bias?

    Answer: Give regional leaders real decision rights and voice

    Inclusion.

    AI-Native Product Organization Design¶

    Q145. What does AI-native org design change?

    Answer: Roles, workflows and skills as AI takes on more production work

    Reimagine.

    Q146. What skill matters more with AI?

    Answer: Judgement, problem framing and evaluation

    Human value.

    Q147. How adopt AI responsibly in product teams?

    Answer: Pilot with guardrails, measure outcomes and manage risk

    Governance.

    Q148. What is a risk?

    Answer: Over-trusting outputs without evaluation

    Verify.

    Legacy: Building Enduring Product Cultures¶

    Q149. What sustains a product culture over years?

    Answer: Systems: hiring, promotion, rituals and leaders who model values

    Reinforced.

    Q150. How refresh culture without losing it?

    Answer: Keep core principles, evolve practices

    Adapt.

    Q151. What erodes culture?

    Answer: Rewarding behavior that contradicts the values

    Incentives.

    Q152. What's a culture health check?

    Answer: Pulse surveys, retros and observing decisions

    Evidence.

    Career Growth: CPO and Beyond¶

    Q153. What develops a CPO?

    Answer: Broad exposure to strategy, finance, people and board work

    Breadth.

    Q154. What is a common next step after CPO?

    Answer: CEO, board roles or founding

    Paths.

    Q155. What helps career growth?

    Answer: Mentors, sponsors and a record of outcomes

    Support.

    Q156. Why keep learning at the top?

    Answer: The context changes; humility drives adaptation

    Growth.

    Capstone — Full CPO-Level Strategy Document¶

    Q157. What does the CPO strategy capstone combine?

    Answer: Strategy, org design, culture, metrics and board narrative

    Level 4 project.

    Q158. How prove strategy is credible?

    Answer: Clear choices, evidence, trade-offs and metrics

    Rigor.

    Q159. What does a CPO narrative include?

    Answer: Where we play, how we win, what we won't do and how we'll measure it

    Clarity.

    Q160. How sustain the strategy?

    Answer: Review regularly against outcomes and adapt

    Learn.

    Mixed questions across all levels¶

    Q161. A PM's ratings are based on 'good attitude' and 'strong communicator'. What is wrong?

    Answer: They are impressions, not behaviours with evidence; use a rubric with two written examples

    Behavioural descriptors.

    Q162. A PM underperformed for three quarters and has never heard it from you. Whose failure is it first?

    ```
# Structured hiring: weighted scorecard, calibration of interviewers, and a hire / no-hire recommendation.
WEIGHTS = {"outcomes": 0.35, "judgement": 0.30, "craft": 0.20, "influence": 0.15}
# scores 1-4 per interviewer (independent, written before the debrief)
FEEDBACK = {
    "Asha":  {"ann": {"outcomes": 4, "judgement": 3, "craft": 3, "influence": 3}, "ben": {"outcomes": 2, "judgement": 2, "craft": 3, "influence": 2}, "cy": {"outcomes": 3, "judgement": 4, "craft": 3, "influence": 4}},
    "Raj":   {"ann": {"outcomes": 3, "judgement": 3, "craft": 4, "influence": 3}, "ben": {"outcomes": 4, "judgement": 4, "craft": 3, "influence": 3}, "cy": {"outcomes": 3, "judgement": 3, "craft": 3, "influence": 3}},
    "Mei":   {"ann": {"outcomes": 4, "judgement": 4, "craft": 3, "influence": 4}, "ben": {"outcomes": 2, "judgement": 1, "craft": 2, "influence": 2}, "cy": {"outcomes": 4, "judgement": 3, "craft": 4, "influence": 3}},
}
BAR = 3.0          # weighted average needed to hire
MAX_SPREAD = 1.5   # interviewers differing by more than this need a calibration conversation

def weighted(scores):
    return sum(scores[k] * w for k, w in WEIGHTS.items())

def evaluate(candidate):
    per = {i: weighted(FEEDBACK[i][candidate]) for i in FEEDBACK}
    avg = sum(per.values()) / len(per)
    spread = max(per.values()) - min(per.values())
    dims = {k: round(sum(FEEDBACK[i][candidate][k] for i in FEEDBACK) / len(FEEDBACK), 2) for k in WEIGHTS}
    weakest = min(dims, key=dims.get)
    if spread > MAX_SPREAD:
        decision = "CALIBRATE"
    elif avg >= BAR and dims[weakest] >= 2.5:
        decision = "HIRE"
    else:
        decision = "NO HIRE"
    return {"avg": round(avg, 2), "spread": round(spread, 2), "weakest": weakest, "decision": decision}

for c in ["ann", "ben", "cy"]:
    r = evaluate(c)
    print(f"{c:4s} avg={r['avg']:.2f} spread={r['spread']:.2f} weakest={r['weakest']:<9s} -> {r['decision']}")
ranking = sorted(["ann", "ben", "cy"], key=lambda c: -evaluate(c)["avg"])
print("ranking:", " > ".join(ranking))
```

Independent written scores avoid anchoring; weighting reflects what the role needs; the spread check catches disagreement that should be discussed rather than averaged away. Verified by running the Python model (the thresholds are illustrative, not a standard).

    ```
# Org capacity model: how many PMs/leads are needed for a set of bets, and what does it cost?
import math
BETS = [  # (name, engineers needed, priority 1=highest)
    ("Checkout redesign", 14, 1), ("Onboarding", 9, 1), ("Search relevance", 11, 2), ("Partner API", 8, 2), ("Mobile app", 15, 3), ("Insights", 6, 3),
]
ENG_PER_PM = 7              # one PM per ~7 engineers (rounded up per bet)
SPAN = 6                    # direct reports per Product Lead
PM_COST, LEAD_COST, RECRUIT = 160_000, 200_000, 0.20     # annual cost and recruiting fee as a share of salary
BUDGET = 1_300_000

def pms_needed(engineers):
    return math.ceil(engineers / ENG_PER_PM)

def leads_needed(pm_count):
    return math.ceil(pm_count / SPAN)

def plan(bets, budget):
    funded, spent, pms = [], 0, 0
    for name, eng, prio in sorted(bets, key=lambda b: (b[2], b[1])):
        need = pms_needed(eng)
        total_pm = pms + need
        cost = total_pm * PM_COST + leads_needed(total_pm) * LEAD_COST
        if cost <= budget:
            funded.append((name, need)); pms, spent = total_pm, cost
    return funded, pms, spent

funded, pms, spent = plan(BETS, BUDGET)
print("funded:", ", ".join(f"{n} ({p} PM)" for n, p in funded))
print("unfunded:", ", ".join(n for n, _, _ in BETS if n not in {f for f, _ in funded}) or "none")
print(f"PMs: {pms}, leads: {leads_needed(pms)}, annual cost: {spent:,}")
print(f"recruiting fees: {round((pms * PM_COST + leads_needed(pms) * LEAD_COST) * RECRUIT):,}")
print(f"headroom: {BUDGET - spent:,}")
for extra in (0, 300_000, 600_000):
    print(f"budget +{extra:,} ->", len(plan(BETS, BUDGET + extra)[0]), "bets funded")
```

Capacity is derived from bets (engineers per PM, PMs per lead) and funded in priority order until the budget binds, making trade-offs explicit. Verified by running the Python model (the ratios and salaries are illustrative assumptions).
